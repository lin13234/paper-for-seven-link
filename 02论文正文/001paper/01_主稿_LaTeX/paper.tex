\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a plane-symmetric spatial 7R closed-chain mechanism for
reconfigurable underactuated locomotion. Screw-system analysis and a modified
Gr\"ubler--Kutzbach criterion show that the mechanism has one global degree of
freedom. A passive-Jacobian formulation combined with singular value
decomposition identifies two one-degree-of-freedom motion branches: a spatial
symmetric branch and a degenerated planar branch. The branches intersect at
singular configurations at which the instantaneous mobility increases and the
active-joint assignment can be changed. Numerical continuation verifies the
branch geometry and distinguishes true singularities from ill-conditioned
non-singular configurations. A four-motor prototype architecture is then
represented in MuJoCo and controlled through a two-stage curriculum-learning
procedure. The first stage discovers the kinematic branches on a test rig and
fits their joint coordination using Fourier series; the second stage uses
these fitted relations as references for ground-locomotion learning. The
resulting policies produce multiple locomotion modes while remaining
consistent with the mechanism-level constraints. Physical experiments are
planned to quantify branch switching and locomotion performance.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item A plane-symmetric spatial 7R mechanism has two one-DOF branches.
\item SVD distinguishes branch singularities from ill-conditioned states.
\item Singular configurations provide deterministic branch-switching gates.
\item Curriculum PPO converts fitted branch coordination into locomotion.
\item Physical prototype tests are specified for subsequent validation.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}

Spatial closed-chain mechanisms combine compact topology, load-bearing
capacity, and constrained spatial motion, making them attractive for
reconfigurable machines and mobile robots. Geometric symmetry can further
organize their configuration spaces and reduce the dimensionality of the
motion that must be generated or controlled. Classical overconstrained
linkages, including Bennett- and Bricard-type mechanisms, illustrate how
carefully selected link dimensions and joint-axis relations can produce
mobility that is not apparent from generic constraint counting.
\missing{Citation needed}

Singular configurations are commonly treated as undesirable because they can
cause rank loss, poor transmission, and large actuator effort. For a
multi-branch mechanism, however, the same configurations may also connect
otherwise distinct components of the configuration space. This dual role
suggests a design strategy in which a singularity is not traversed
accidentally but used as a predictable gateway for selecting a motion branch.
The main challenge is to establish that the branches are genuine
one-degree-of-freedom motions, identify their intersections, and show that a
physical actuation architecture can reliably excite them.
\missing{Citation needed}

Existing studies of spatial and overconstrained linkages have extensively
addressed mobility, bifurcation, and singularity. In contrast, the connection
between mechanism-level branch geometry and learned locomotion remains less
developed. In particular, direct reinforcement learning under ground contact
mixes kinematic coordination, contact dynamics, and propulsion in one
optimization problem. Such coupling makes it difficult to determine whether a
learned gait reflects the predicted mechanism branch or merely a
contact-dependent local strategy.
\missing{Citation needed: prior work on reconfigurable linkages and learned locomotion}

Here, we propose a plane-symmetric spatial 7R closed-chain mechanism and use
its singular configurations as deterministic gateways between a spatial
symmetric branch and a degenerated planar branch. We first establish its
global mobility, formulate its passive Jacobian, and derive an SVD-based
criterion for branch singularities. Numerical continuation then characterizes
the two branches and their intersections. Finally, a redundantly actuated
prototype is evaluated in a MuJoCo-based curriculum: branch coordination is
first learned on a test rig and fitted using Fourier series, after which the
fitted relations guide locomotion learning on the ground. The present
manuscript reports the theoretical and simulation framework; quantitative
physical-prototype validation is retained as a planned experimental stage.

\section{Kinematic Modeling and Reconfiguration Analysis}

\subsection{Mechanism architecture and geometric parameters}

The proposed mechanism is a spatial single-loop chain containing seven
revolute joints. Its geometry is symmetric with respect to a plane that maps
the corresponding links and joint axes on the two sides of the chain. The
joint variables are denoted by \(\mathbf q=[q_1,q_2,\ldots,q_7]^T\). Owing
to the plane-symmetric architecture, Joints~1, 2, 3, and 7 can each be
selected as a kinematic input. To avoid redundant calculations, only one
representative coordinate is used in each numerical continuation analysis.
For the explicit branch-curve derivation below, the ordered program indices
\((1,3,5,7)\) are relabeled as the physical sequence \((2,4,6,1)\) so that
the final notation remains consistent with the numerical plots and the
corrected joint numbering. This computational choice does not restrict the
physical actuation arrangement. In the prototype, the four candidate input
joints are organized into the two symmetric motor groups
\(\{\mathrm{J1},\mathrm{J2}\}\) and \(\{\mathrm{J3},\mathrm{J7}\}\);
selecting a single input in the kinematic calculation therefore represents a
parameterization choice rather than a reduction of the installed actuation.

Equivalently, the non-redundant geometric relations are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\alpha_0=0^\circ,\qquad
\alpha_1=\alpha_2=\alpha_3=\alpha_4=\alpha_5=\alpha_6=90^\circ,\\
&a_0=a_6=2l,\quad a_1=a_4=2.5l,\quad a_2=a_5=0,\quad a_3=l,\\
&d_2=d_6=2.5l,\qquad
 d_1=d_3=d_4=d_5=d_7=0,\\
&\theta_{3,0}=\theta_{6,0}=180^\circ,\qquad
\theta_{i,0}=0^\circ\quad(i\neq3,6).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:design_parameters}
\end{equation}
In particular, the zero pair \(a_2=0\) and \(d_3=0\) permits the chosen
frames around Joints~3 and~4 to share the same origin in the corrected frame
assignment, while \(\alpha_2=90^\circ\) indicates that the corresponding
joint axes remain orthogonal. This MDH model supplies both the finite
loop-closure transformations and the joint-axis geometry required for the
screw-system analysis below.

\subsection{Screw-system-based mobility analysis}

The joint twists are expressed in a common reference frame as
\(\mathbf S_i=[\boldsymbol{\omega}_i^T,\mathbf v_i^T]^T\), where
\(\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\) is the unit direction of the \(i\)th revolute axis
and \(\mathbf v_i=-\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\times\mathbf r_i\):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf S_i=
\begin{bmatrix}
\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\\
-\boldsymbol{\omega}_i\times\mathbf r_i
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:screw_definition}
\end{equation}
Starting from \(\mathbf S_i^{(i)}=[0,0,1,0,0,0]^T\) in the local joint frame,
the global screw is obtained through
\begin{equation}
\mathbf S_i=\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathbf T_{0,i-1}}\mathbf S_i^{(i)},\qquad
\operatorname{Ad}_{\mathbf T}=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf R&\mathbf 0\\
[\mathbf p]_{\times}\mathbf R&\mathbf R
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:adjoint_mapping}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf T=[\mathbf R,\mathbf p;\mathbf 0^T,1]\).

Representative screws from the current symbolic model are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf S_2&=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\1\\0\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},&
\mathbf S_3&=\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\1\\0\\-l\\0\end{bmatrix},\\
\mathbf S_4&=\begin{bmatrix}
\sin q_2\\-\cos q_2\\0\\0\\0\\-a-l\cos q_2
\end{bmatrix},&
\mathbf S_5&=\begin{bmatrix}
\sin q_3\cos q_2\\
\sin q_2\sin q_3\\
-\cos q_3\\
(-a\sin q_2+b\cos q_2)\cos q_3\\
(a\cos q_2+b\sin q_2+l)\cos q_3\\
(b+l\sin q_2)\sin q_3
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:representative_screws}
\end{equation}
Here, \(l\) is the shortest link length, with \(a=2l\) and \(b=2.5l\) used
as the reduced symbolic parameters in the explicit branch derivation.

Collecting the twists gives the global Jacobian
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J=
\begin{bmatrix}
|&|&&|\\
\mathbf S_1&\mathbf S_2&\cdots&\mathbf S_7\\
|&|&&|
\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{6\times7}.
\label{eq:global_jacobian}
\end{equation}
Solving the reciprocal condition
\(\mathbf J^T\mathbf S^r=\mathbf 0\) under the plane-symmetric geometry yields
no non-zero common reciprocal screw. The number of common constraints is
therefore \(\lambda=0\), and the order of the mechanism is
\begin{equation}
d=6-\lambda=6.
\end{equation}
For \(n=7\) links, \(g=7\) one-degree-of-freedom revolute joints, and
\(\sum f_i=7\), the modified Gr\"ubler--Kutzbach criterion gives
\begin{equation}
M=d(n-g-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{g}f_i
  =6(7-7-1)+7=1.
\label{eq:mobility}
\end{equation}
Thus, the mechanism has one global degree of freedom away from singular
configurations. The complete twist expressions are reserved for the
supplementary derivation because their length obscures the mobility argument.
This mobility count establishes the dimension of regular motion but does not
identify how the joint coordinates are coupled or where that coupling ceases
to be locally unique. These questions are addressed through the differentiated
loop-closure equations.

\subsection{Closed-loop kinematic formulation}

Let \(\mathbf T_i(\mathbf q)\in\mathrm{SE}(3)\) denote the relative
transformation associated with the structural and joint transformations at
Joint \(i\). Loop closure requires
\begin{equation}
\mathbf T_1\mathbf T_2\mathbf T_3\mathbf T_4
\mathbf T_5\mathbf T_6\mathbf T_7=\mathbf I.
\label{eq:loop_closure}
\end{equation}
Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:loop_closure} gives the velocity constraint
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J\dot{\mathbf q}=\mathbf 0.
\label{eq:velocity_constraint}
\end{equation}
For one actuated coordinate \(q_a\), the velocity vector is partitioned into
the actuated and passive coordinates, which yields
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J_p\dot{\mathbf q}_p=-\mathbf J_a\dot q_a.
\label{eq:passive_jacobian}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\mathbf J_p\in\mathbb R^{6\times6}\) is obtained by removing the
column associated with the selected input from \(\mathbf J\). Its
invertibility determines whether the passive coordinates are locally unique
functions of the input coordinate.

For the representative continuation of the spatial symmetric branch,
Joint~3 is selected as the computational input, and the matrix blocks are
explicitly
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J_a=\mathbf S_3,\qquad
\mathbf J_p=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf S_1&\mathbf S_2&\mathbf S_4&
\mathbf S_5&\mathbf S_6&\mathbf S_7
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:jacobian_partition_spatial}
\end{equation}
For the representative continuation of the degenerated planar branch,
Joint~2 is selected instead:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J_a=\mathbf S_2,\qquad
\mathbf J_p=
\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf S_1&\mathbf S_3&\mathbf S_4&
\mathbf S_5&\mathbf S_6&\mathbf S_7
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:jacobian_partition_planar}
\end{equation}
The two partitions describe the same closed chain under different local
parameterizations. They are retained separately because each one is used to
continue a different motion branch from the shared singular configurations.

\subsection{SVD-based singularity analysis}

The passive Jacobian is decomposed as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J_p=\mathbf U\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\mathbf V^T,\qquad
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}=\operatorname{diag}
(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\ldots,\sigma_6),
\label{eq:svd}
\end{equation}
where \(\sigma_1\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_6\geq0\). A true kinematic singularity
occurs when
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf J_p)<6
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf J_p)=\sigma_6=0.
\label{eq:singularity_criterion}
\end{equation}
The number of zero singular values gives the dimension added to the
instantaneous null space. Accordingly, a rank loss by one raises the
instantaneous mobility from one to two at a candidate branch intersection.
The SVD is therefore used as a local rank-loss test, whereas numerical
continuation of both branches is used in Section~3 to confirm that the
rank-deficient configuration is also a global manifold intersection.

In numerical calculations, a small singular value alone is insufficient to
establish a bifurcation. A configuration is classified as ill-conditioned or
pseudo-singular when \(\sigma_{\min}\) has a local minimum but remains
strictly non-zero. The condition number
\begin{equation}
\kappa(\mathbf J_p)=\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_6}
\end{equation}
is used as a complementary measure of local sensitivity and transmission
deterioration. This distinction prevents the ill-conditioned configurations
near \(90^\circ\) and \(270^\circ\) from being misidentified as branch
intersections.

\subsection{Branch reconfiguration at singular configurations}

Along the spatial symmetric branch, the joint variables satisfy
\begin{equation}
q_1=q_2,\qquad q_7=-q_3,\qquad q_6=-q_4.
\label{eq:spatial_symmetry}
\end{equation}
At a true singular configuration, the joint axes separate into the mutually
orthogonal groups \(\{3,5,7\}\) and \(\{1,2,4,6\}\). Holding Joints
\(\{3,5,7\}\) fixed leaves Joints \(\{1,2,4,6\}\) to form an equivalent
planar four-bar chain. The rank-deficient configuration consequently admits
two locally allowable departure directions: one continues the spatial
symmetric motion, whereas the other enters the degenerated planar motion.
Exciting the corresponding joint group selects which tangent direction is
followed, providing the kinematic basis for branch reconfiguration.

\subsection{Explicit branch parameterization and joint-angle fitting}

On the selected branch, the motion can be written as an explicit parametric
curve. Let
\[
l_0=l,\qquad a=2l,\qquad b=2.5l,\qquad
l_1=l_3=\sqrt{a^2+b^2},\qquad l_2=2a+l=5l,
\]
and define
\[
\beta=\operatorname{atan2}(b,a).
\]
Using the branch parameter \(\theta_1\), the intermediate angles are
\[
\phi_1=\theta_1-\beta,
\]
\[
\mathbf p_3=
\begin{bmatrix}
l_1\cos\phi_1\\
l_1\sin\phi_1
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
d=\sqrt{(l_1\cos\phi_1-l_0)^2+(l_1\sin\phi_1)^2}.
\]
The geometric angle at the closure triangle is
\[
\alpha=\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(l_1\sin\phi_1,\;l_1\cos\phi_1-l_0\right),
\]
and the law of cosines gives
\[
\gamma=\arccos\!\left(
\frac{l_3^2+d^2-l_2^2}{2l_3d}
\right).
\]
Hence,
\[
\phi_3=\alpha-\gamma,
\]
and the remaining coordination relations are
\[
\theta_3=\theta_1-\phi_2+\pi,\qquad
\theta_5=\phi_3-\phi_2-\beta,\qquad
\theta_7=\pi+\beta-\phi_3,
\]
where
\[
\phi_2=
\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(
 l_3\sin\phi_3-l_1\sin\phi_1,\;
 l_0+l_3\cos\phi_3-l_1\cos\phi_1
\right).
\]
This provides a closed-form parametric representation of the selected
branch. When \(\theta_7\) is prescribed as the input, the inverse mapping
\(\theta_1(\theta_7)\) is recovered numerically on the same branch by
minimizing the wrapped angular residual
\[
\Delta(\theta_1)=
\operatorname{atan2}\!\Big(
\sin(\theta_7(\theta_1)-\theta_7^\star),\;
\cos(\theta_7(\theta_1)-\theta_7^\star)
\Big),
\qquad
\theta_1^\star=\arg\min_{\theta_1}\Delta(\theta_1)^2.
\]
The converged solution from the previous sample is used as the initial guess
for the next sample, which preserves branch continuity during the sweep.

After numerical continuation, the joint trajectories are unwrapped and
referenced to the initial pose,
\[
\Delta\theta_i=\operatorname{unwrap}(\theta_i)-
\operatorname{unwrap}(\theta_i)\big|_{\theta_7=\theta_{7,0}},
\qquad i\in\{1,3,5,7\}.
\]
The periodic coordination is then approximated by a truncated Fourier
series,
\[
\widehat{\Delta\theta_i}(\Delta\theta_7)=c_{i,0}+
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Big[
 c_{i,k}\cos(k\Delta\theta_7)+s_{i,k}\sin(k\Delta\theta_7)
\Big].
\]
The coefficients are obtained by least squares on the unwrapped samples, and
the resulting fitted curves are directly reused in the curriculum-learning
stage.

\section{Numerical Validation of the Two Motion Branches}

\subsection{Numerical continuation and singularity identification}

For a prescribed input angle, loop closure is solved by minimizing a
six-dimensional residual containing the position and orientation errors of
the terminal frame. A BFGS quasi-Newton solver searches for the passive joint
coordinates until the residual norm is below \(10^{-4}\). The converged
solution at the preceding input step initializes the next solve, thereby
providing numerical continuation on one assembly branch and suppressing
unintended jumps between branches.

With Joint~3 as the input, the singular-value spectrum is evaluated over a
complete revolution. The available results identify true rank-deficient
configurations near \(q_3=0^\circ\) and \(q_3=180^\circ\);
\(q_3=360^\circ\) is physically identical to \(q_3=0^\circ\). Local minima
near \(90^\circ\) and \(270^\circ\) remain non-zero and are therefore
classified as ill-conditioned configurations rather than additional
singularities.

\subsection{Spatial symmetric branch}

The continued solutions form a smooth one-dimensional trajectory parameterized
by \(q_3\). Pairwise comparisons of the joint coordinates confirm the
symmetry relations in Eq.~\eqref{eq:spatial_symmetry}. Fourier series provide
a compact representation of the numerical joint trajectories,

\hat q_i(q_3)=c_{i,0}+

\label{eq:fourier_general}

used as numerical surrogates and later as coordination references for

\section{Prototype Design and Curriculum-Learning-Based PPO Validation}

\subsection{Prototype and simulation framework}

The mechanism is embodied as a four-motor mobile prototype. The chassis and
major links are manufactured by three-dimensional printing using
polylactic-acid/carbon-fibre (PLA--CF) composite, while the left and right
ground-contact links provide the primary propulsive interactions. The motors
are arranged in symmetric pairs so that different joint groups can excite the
spatial branch, the planar branch, or a transition strategy. Redundant
actuation is used because a single compact motor does not provide sufficient
torque margin near poorly conditioned configurations.

The MuJoCo virtual prototype reproduces the CAD geometry, joint locations,
motion limits, masses, inertias, and contact bodies of the physical design.
This correspondence is necessary because the PPO policies are intended for
the manufactured mechanism rather than for an abstract kinematic chain.

\subsection{Stage I: discovery of kinematic motion branches on the test rig}

In the first curriculum stage, the virtual prototype is fixed to a test rig.
Removing gross body motion and ground contact isolates the coordination
problem of the closed-chain mechanism. Under a common reward definition,
random seed, and PPO configuration, the six possible two-motor combinations
are compared as a redundant-actuation ablation. They are grouped into
plane-symmetric, spatial-symmetric, and asymmetric mixed actuation according
to the selected joints.

The reward promotes the prescribed input motion, loop-compatible joint
coordination, low actuation effort, and smooth actions. The ablation curves
are used to compare convergence and the final motion branch rather than to
claim that one policy is universally superior. Available training runs
contain policies corresponding to the spatial symmetric branch, the
degenerated planar branch, and branch-changing behavior near a singular
configuration.

\subsection{Stage II: Fourier-guided locomotion learning on the ground}

In the second stage, the test rig is removed and the robot is placed on the
ground. The main input joint follows a prescribed periodic command, and the
remaining joint coordinates are encouraged to track the Fourier reference
obtained in Stage~I. A general reward form is
\begin{equation}
r=w_v r_{\mathrm{velocity}}
 +w_k r_{\mathrm{kinematic}}
 +w_s r_{\mathrm{stability}}
 -w_e r_{\mathrm{energy}}
 -w_a r_{\mathrm{action}},
\label{eq:locomotion_reward}
\end{equation}
where \(r_{\mathrm{kinematic}}\) penalizes deviations from the branch-specific
Fourier coordination. The other terms promote propulsion and stable,
energy-aware, smooth behavior under contact.

This curriculum separates two questions that are entangled in direct
end-to-end training: whether the actuators can realize a valid kinematic
branch, and whether that branch can generate useful ground motion. The current
simulations produced spatial full-rotation crawling, spatial peristaltic
crawling, and planar peristaltic crawling policies, including forward and
reverse motion. Their quantitative comparison remains dependent on a unified
evaluation protocol.

\section{Discussion}

The principal mechanism-level result is that singularity and reconfiguration
are two aspects of the same configuration-space structure. Rank loss in the
passive Jacobian marks configurations at which the normal one-dimensional
motion ceases to be locally unique, while the orthogonal joint-axis groups
provide two admissible directions for leaving the intersection. The
singularity is therefore not merely a failure state; under controlled
actuation it becomes a branch-selection gateway.

The learning results also indicate a useful division of labour between
mechanical structure and control. The closed-chain topology restricts the
admissible coordination before learning begins. Stage~I identifies which
redundant motor groups can excite each branch, and the Fourier fit transfers
that coordination to Stage~II as a reference. PPO is then used primarily to
resolve contact, propulsion, energy, and stability within the mechanically
admissible motion, rather than to rediscover the entire closed-chain
kinematics under contact.

Several boundaries remain. The planar-branch signed angle relations require a
final notation check, and the current simulation results need repeated-seed
statistics and uniform gait metrics. More importantly, an accurately
parameterized virtual prototype does not by itself establish transfer to the
manufactured system. Joint clearance, compliance, actuator saturation,
surface friction, and impact may change the attainable switching region and
locomotion efficiency. For this reason, the physical experiments in
Section~5 are required before making claims about real-world robustness,
zero-shot generalization, steering, or terrain adaptation.

\section{Conclusions}

This study develops a plane-symmetric spatial 7R closed-chain mechanism whose
configuration space contains a spatial symmetric branch and a degenerated
planar branch. Screw-system analysis and the modified
Gr\"ubler--Kutzbach criterion establish one global degree of freedom, whereas
SVD of the passive Jacobian identifies the singular configurations at which
instantaneous mobility increases and branch selection becomes possible.
Numerical continuation verifies the two branches and separates true
singularities from ill-conditioned non-singular states. A two-stage
curriculum then uses test-rig PPO training to discover branch-specific
coordination and Fourier-guided ground training to generate multiple
locomotion modes. These results support the use of mechanism topology as a
structural prior for learned locomotion, while quantitative physical
prototype experiments remain necessary to establish real-world performance.
